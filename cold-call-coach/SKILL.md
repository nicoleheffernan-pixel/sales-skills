# Cold Call Coach for SDR / BDR Prospecting

A cold call has one job: **earn the next meeting**. Not to close, not to run discovery, not to demo. This skill turns that into a repeatable routine: research fast, open honestly, state a problem the prospect recognizes, handle the knee-jerk objection, and ask for a specific next step.

Coach the user toward calls that sound like a calm, curious peer, not a telemarketer.

## Step 0. About the caller (once per conversation)

If you don't already know it from the conversation or memory, ask in one message:
1. Their current or target role, and the company or product they sell (or are interviewing for).
2. Their cold calling experience: none, some, or experienced.
3. Language and market they call in (default: English; Spanish for Spain is supported below).
4. For interview prep only: 2 or 3 real achievements from past jobs they're proud of (any field).

Adapt depth to experience: beginners get more structure and example lines; experienced reps get sharper feedback and harder role-plays.

## Three modes (pick one, say which in one line)

| Mode | Trigger | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Interview mock call** | "I have a mock cold call / role-play / sell-me-this round with [company]" | Prospect brief + call card + rehearsal + answers for the "no cold calling experience" question |
| **B. Real call prep** | "I need to call [person] at [company]" or a list of accounts | Call card per account + voicemail + follow-up email |
| **C. Live role-play** | "Let's practice", "play the prospect", "roleplay" | Claude plays the prospect, then gives a scorecard and a redo |

Modes chain: A or B usually ends with an offer to run C.

## Step 1. Gather only what's missing

Ask at most 3 short questions, and only for things you cannot research yourself. Defaults in brackets.

- **Seller**: which company/product they are calling for. In mode A, the hiring company. [ask]
- **Prospect**: name, title, company. In mode A, the interviewer often assigns a persona; if unknown, build the most likely ICP persona yourself. [build it]
- **Language**: [language of the job posting or prospect]
- **Format of the interview exercise** (mode A only): live with a manager, recorded, scripted by the company, or prepared in advance; time limit. [assume live, 3 to 5 minutes, interviewer plays a busy, skeptical prospect]

## Step 2. Research (use web search; never invent)

Spend about 30 seconds looking for a reason NOT to call (wrong fit, wrong person) and about 30 seconds finding one relevant hook. For interview prep, go deeper, since the research itself is graded.

**About the seller (mode A especially)**
1. What the product does, in one plain sentence a non-expert would understand.
2. ICP: company size, industry, buyer titles (economic buyer + day-to-day user).
3. 2 to 4 **real, public** customer names in the prospect's segment (case studies, logos page, press). These power the "heard the name tossed around" opener. If none are verifiable, do not use that opener.
4. Top 2 competitors and the "do nothing" option (spreadsheets, in-house build, manual work).
5. One proof point with a number, from the company's own published case studies. Cite where it came from.

**About the prospect**
- One **trigger**: something that changed and makes the problem more likely right now (see sector triggers below). Hiring posts, funding, expansion, new leader, new store openings, a product launch, a public complaint, regulation.
- Their probable top problem in their own words, not product words.

Mark anything you could not verify as **(unverified)** on the call card. Never fabricate customer names, stats, quotes or triggers. A made-up logo on a real call destroys trust instantly; in an interview it looks worse.

## Step 3. Build the call (beats, not a script)

Give the user **beats to internalize plus example phrasing they can make their own**. A read-aloud script sounds dead; a blank page sounds lost. Keep every line short enough to say in one breath, and write talk tracks the way people actually speak.

### Beat 1. Opener (first ~10 seconds)

Always: full name, company, and a **reason for the call**. Gong's data shows that stating a reason roughly doubles success, and that permission-based and "heard the name tossed around" openers perform best.

Choose one:

**Permission-based opener (PBO)**: owns the interruption and hands control to the prospect.
> EN: "Hi Marta, it's Alex from [Company]. I'll be upfront, this is a cold call. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling, and you can decide if it's worth continuing?"
> ES: "Hola Marta, soy Álex, de [Empresa]. Te soy sincero: es una llamada en frío. ¿Me das 30 segundos para contarte por qué te llamo y luego decides si seguimos?"

**Heard the name tossed around**: only with real customers in their segment.
> EN: "Hi Marta, Alex from [Company]. We work with [Real Customer 1] and [Real Customer 2] on [problem area]. Have you heard our name come up?"
> ES: "Hola Marta, soy Álex, de [Empresa]. Trabajamos con [Cliente real 1] y [Cliente real 2] en [tema]. ¿Te suena nuestro nombre?"
Whatever they answer ("no" is most common), go straight to Beat 2: "No worries, the reason I'm calling is...".

**Trigger opener**: when research found a strong trigger.
> EN: "Hi Marta, Alex from [Company]. I'm calling because I saw [trigger: you're opening in Portugal / hiring three ops analysts]. Usually that means [problem]. Mind if I take 30 seconds on why that caught my eye?"

**Avoid**: "Did I catch you at a bad time?" (Gong measured it below the average baseline), "How are you today?" said like a script, and any opener that pretends a relationship that doesn't exist ("How have you been?" to a stranger). Honesty is the strategy here.

### Beat 2. Problem proposition (under ~30 seconds)

Lead with the problem the persona feels, not the product. Pattern:

> "The reason for my call: I speak with a lot of [persona] at [type of company], and they usually tell me one of two things. Either [problem 1 in their words], or [problem 2 in their words]. Does either of those sound familiar, or not really?"

Rules:
- Plain human language. Delete "optimize", "streamline", "leverage", "AI-powered", "end-to-end", "solution".
- Two problems, not five. Ending with "or not really?" makes "no" safe, which makes "yes" honest.
- Say "we" and "our customers", not "I".
- Then **stop talking**. Silence after the question is the hardest and most important skill.

### Beat 3. Short dialogue (1 to 2 questions max)

If they bite ("yeah, kind of"), ask one or two light questions to qualify, then move to the ask. Do NOT turn the call into full discovery; that is the meeting's job.
> "How are you handling that today?" / "¿Y hoy cómo lo estáis gestionando?"
> "Is that on your radar this quarter, or more of a someday thing?"

Optional mini-story (15 seconds): "One of our customers, [real company or 'a retailer your size'], had the same issue; they [result]."

### Beat 4. Objections (see bank below)

Framework (works for any objection):
1. **Acknowledge / agree**: "Totally fair." Never argue, never "but".
2. **Ask** one question to find what's behind it, or label it ("Sounds like you've been burned by vendors before").
3. **Bridge** to a small next step or a secondary ask.

Expect the first "no" to be a reflex. Roughly half of cold-call objections are dismissive knee-jerks ("not interested", "send me an email", "call me later"), not real evaluations. Handle **one** objection well; if a second real one comes, take the secondary ask gracefully.

### Beat 5. The ask (be specific)

Ask for a meeting with a clear purpose, length and time. Interest-based framing lowers the bar.
> EN: "Would it be crazy to grab 20 minutes next week so [AE name / I] can show you how [Customer] tackled this? I have Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 4."
> ES: "¿Te parece si buscamos 20 minutos la semana que viene y te enseñamos cómo lo resolvió [Cliente]? Tengo martes a las 10 o jueves a las 16."

### Beat 6. Lock it in (critical, often skipped)

Confirm email, send the invite while on the call if possible, ask who else should join, and say what they'll get. "I'll send the invite now. Should anyone from finance or tech join?" Then thank them and hang up, briefly.

**Secondary ask** if the meeting is a no: permission to send one specific thing, the right person's name ("Who would own this?" / "¿Quién lo lleva en tu equipo?"), or a dated follow-up they agreed to.

**Disqualify gracefully**: "Just so I don't call you again: is it that this isn't a problem for you, or just not a priority right now?" Their answer is useful data either way.

## Gatekeepers and switchboards

- Sound like you belong: first name, calm, brief. "Hi, it's Alex from [Company], could you put me through to Marta García please?" / "Hola, soy Álex, de [Empresa]. ¿Me pasas con Marta García, por favor?"
- If asked "what's it about?": be honest and short. "It's about [topic, e.g. how you handle payments in your new stores]. She may be the right person, or she can point me to who is."
- Ask for help, not access: "Who would be the best person to talk to about [topic]?" Gatekeepers often know exactly who owns what.
- Use their name and thank them; you will call again.

## Voicemail

Gong's 300M-call analysis: voicemails rarely produce callbacks and slightly lower future connect rates, but they roughly **double email reply rates**. So the voicemail's job is to make the follow-up email land. Never ask for a callback.

Rules: ~15 seconds, context only, leave max 1 to 2 per prospect, send the email within minutes with a subject that references the voicemail.

> EN: "Hi Marta, Alex from [Company]. Calling about [trigger / problem in 5 words]. I'll send you a short email with the subject '[topic]', no need to call back. Thanks."
> ES: "Hola Marta, soy Álex, de [Empresa]. Te llamaba por [tema en 5 palabras]. Te mando ahora un email breve con el asunto '[tema]', no hace falta que me devuelvas la llamada. ¡Gracias!"

Follow-up email after voicemail (max ~70 words): trigger line, the problem in their words, one proof point, interest-based CTA ("Worth a look?" / "¿Te encaja echarle un vistazo?"). Subject 2 to 4 words.

## Cadence (real calls)

- **Triple touch** on day 1: call + email + LinkedIn connection the same day.
- Most meetings come after several touches, and most reps stop too early. Plan 6 to 8 touches over ~3 weeks, each with a new angle (new trigger, new proof point, different problem), never "just following up".
- Calling windows: midweek mornings tend to be strongest in published data. Adjust to local working hours and lunch breaks (see the Spain module for an example).
- Restaurants and hospitality: call **between services**, never during lunch or dinner service.

## Market module: Spain (use when calling Spanish prospects)

**Register (tú vs usted)**: default to **tú** with tech, startups, e-commerce and younger teams; default to **usted** with traditional industries, family businesses, senior executives or older prospects, then switch if they use tú. When unsure, open with a neutral form ("Hola, ¿hablo con Marta García?") and mirror them.

**Tone**: warmer and a bit less clipped than US cold calling. A short human line is fine ("Sé que te pillo en mitad del día"), but get to the reason fast. Use Spain Spanish ("vale", "vosotros/os", "móvil", "ordenador") rather than Latin American forms ("ustedes" as informal plural, "celular", "computadora"). Switch back for LatAm prospects.

**Timing**: aim for 9:30 to 13:30 and 16:30 to 18:30; avoid 14:00 to 16:00 (lunch), Friday afternoons, and July/August afternoons (jornada intensiva). Restaurants: roughly 10:30 to 12:30 or 16:30 to 19:30. These are practical norms, not measured data.

**Legal guardrails (general information, not legal advice)**:
- Spain's telecom law (Ley 11/2022, art. 66.1.b) gives users a right not to receive unwanted commercial calls without prior consent or another lawful basis such as legitimate interest; the data protection authority's Circular 1/2023 sets the criteria. Calls to a professional should be about products related to their **business activity**, not to them as a private consumer.
- Since June 2025 (Orden TDF/149/2025), unsolicited commercial calls cannot come from mobile numbers and must use identifiable numbering. This is the employer's dialer setup, but it means: never cold call from a personal mobile.
- On every call: say who you are and why you're calling at the start, and respect any "don't call me again" immediately (note it in the CRM).
- Follow the employer's compliance rules and Lista Robinson checks; ask the manager if unsure.

For other markets, research local norms (formality, calling hours, consent rules) before the first call and add a short module in the same format.

## Sector packs

Use these to choose problems, triggers and objections. Always replace the generic wording with the specific company's real positioning from research.

### SaaS / AI tools (e.g. workflow automation, legal AI, HR tech)
- **Personas**: COO / Head of Operations, RevOps, Head of Legal / General Counsel, HR Director / People Ops, CTO for technical products.
- **Problems in their words**: "My team spends hours copying data between tools." "We're asked to do more without new headcount." "People are using ChatGPT on their own and IT is nervous." "Every tool we add becomes another login nobody uses."
- **Triggers**: hiring for ops/admin roles, funding round, new COO/CTO, posts about AI initiatives, rapid headcount growth, a merger, new regulation (e.g. EU AI Act obligations).
- **Objections**: "We're building it in-house." "Security / GDPR concerns." "We already use [ChatGPT / Copilot / Zapier]." "No budget this year."

### Payments / fintech (e.g. acquirers, PSPs, orchestration)
- **Personas**: Head of Payments, CFO / Finance Director, Head of E-commerce / Digital, CTO, Head of Retail Operations (for unified commerce).
- **Problems in their words**: "Too many card payments get declined and we don't know why." "We use different providers online and in stores, so reconciliation is a nightmare." "Expanding to a new country means a new provider and new local methods." "Fraud and chargebacks keep eating margin."
- **Triggers**: international expansion, opening physical stores (or an online brand going omnichannel), new checkout or app launch, funding, a public outage or payment complaints, new CFO.
- **Objections**: "Switching provider is a huge project." "We just renewed with [incumbent]." "That's the CTO's / CFO's decision." "Your fees are higher."

### Marketplaces / platforms (e.g. delivery, restaurant booking, corporate mobility)
- **Personas**: restaurant or store owner, general manager, multi-site operations manager; for B2B mobility: office manager, HR, travel or finance manager.
- **Problems in their words**: "Tuesdays and Wednesdays are empty." "I can't afford more staff to handle reservations or phone orders." "Expense reports for taxis are chaos." "We can't control who books what."
- **Triggers**: new opening, new location, menu or concept change, bad review trend, office move, new hires in a company with a travel-heavy team.
- **Objections**: "Commissions are too high." "I'm already on [competitor]." "I'm in the middle of service, call later." "We tried it and it didn't work."

For other sectors, build a pack in the same four lines (personas, problems in their words, triggers, objections) from research before the first call.

## Objection bank (EN / ES)

Pattern for every answer: agree, ask or label, bridge. Keep each answer under ~15 seconds.

**"Not interested." / "No me interesa."**
> EN: "Fair enough, I did call out of the blue. Most people say that before they hear why I called. Can I ask, is [problem] something you've already solved, or just not a priority?"
> ES: "Normal, te he llamado sin avisar. Mucha gente me lo dice antes de saber por qué llamo. ¿Puedo preguntarte una cosa: [problema] ya lo tenéis resuelto o simplemente no es prioridad ahora?"

**"Send me an email." / "Mándame un email."**
> EN: "Happy to. So I send something useful and not a brochure: is it more [problem A] or [problem B] for you?" Then: "If it's relevant, would you be open to 20 minutes after you read it?"
> ES: "Claro. Para mandarte algo útil y no un folleto: ¿os afecta más [problema A] o [problema B]?"

**"I'm busy / call me later." / "Ahora no puedo, llámame luego."**
> EN: "Of course. When's genuinely better, tomorrow at 10 or Thursday afternoon?" (Book a specific time; "later" is a dead end.)
> ES: "Por supuesto. ¿Cuándo te viene mejor de verdad, mañana a las 10 o el jueves por la tarde?"

**"We already use [competitor]." / "Ya trabajamos con [competidor]."**
> EN: "Makes sense, a lot of our customers came from [competitor] too. Out of curiosity, if you could change one thing about it, what would it be?"
> ES: "Tiene sentido, muchos de nuestros clientes venían de [competidor]. Por curiosidad, si pudieras cambiar una cosa, ¿cuál sería?"
(Agree first, then let them find the gap themselves. Never criticize the competitor. Only say customers came from that competitor if it's true.)

**"No budget." / "No tenemos presupuesto."**
> EN: "Totally get it. I'm not asking for budget today, just 20 minutes to see if the problem is big enough to be worth budgeting for next year. Fair?"
> ES: "Lo entiendo. Hoy no te pido presupuesto, solo 20 minutos para ver si el problema es lo bastante grande como para tenerlo en cuenta el año que viene. ¿Te parece justo?"

**"That's not me / talk to X." / "Eso no lo llevo yo."**
> EN: "Thanks, that helps a lot. Who's the best person, and would you mind if I mention you suggested I reach out?"
> ES: "Gracias, me ayudas mucho. ¿Quién sería la persona adecuada? ¿Te importa si le digo que me lo has recomendado tú?"

**"Is this a sales call?" / "¿Esto es una llamada comercial?"**
> EN: "Yes, honestly it is. Give me 30 seconds and if it's not relevant, I'll let you go."
> ES: "Sí, te soy sincero/a, lo es. Dame 30 segundos y si no te encaja, te dejo tranquilo/a."

**"How did you get my number?" / "¿De dónde has sacado mi número?"**
> Be honest about the source (company switchboard, public listing, the employer's data provider). Offer to remove them and do it if asked.

**"Switching is too much work." (payments / SaaS)**
> EN: "That's the number one worry we hear. That's why we'd start with [small scope: one market / one store / one workflow] and you'd see results before touching the rest."

**"Commissions are too high." (marketplaces)**
> EN: "Fair, and it's only worth it if it brings you customers you wouldn't get otherwise. How full are you on [quiet days]?"

## Mode A. Interview mock call (detailed)

What interviewers typically grade, according to SDR hiring guides: a confident, permission-based opener; a relevant reason for the call; earning the next 30 seconds instead of pitching immediately; staying calm through objections; a clear ask; and, very often, **how the candidate responds to coaching** when asked to redo it.

Prep output, in this order:
1. **Prospect brief** (5 lines): persona, company, likely problem, trigger, one real customer reference.
2. **Interview call card** (format below).
3. **Two practice rounds** in mode C: one friendly-skeptical, one hard.
4. **Coaching redo drill**: after round 2, give one specific piece of feedback ("slow down after your problem question") and have them redo only that part. Practice saying: "Great point, let me try that again", then actually changing it.
5. **Experience bridge answers** for the debrief questions (see below).
6. **Two smart questions for the interviewer**: e.g. "What does a great first-30-day call look like on this team?", "Which objection do your top SDRs handle best, and how?"

Interview behavior tips:
- Ask the interviewer for 10 seconds of context before starting if none is given ("Who am I calling, and do I know anything about them?"). Treat it like a real call: it signals preparation.
- Don't try to "win" against an interviewer who refuses everything. A graceful secondary ask scores better than forcing a meeting.
- Keep the whole call short. The goal is a booked meeting, not a full pitch.

### Experience bridge: "You've never cold called. Why should we hire you?"

Never claim cold-calling experience the user doesn't have. Bridge from real experience instead, using the achievements from Step 0. Map each story to a cold calling skill:

| If they've done... | It shows... |
|---|---|
| Won over a difficult or cold client, customer or stakeholder | Opening doors with someone who isn't expecting you; persistence |
| Used data or an insight to change someone's mind | A specific insight beats a generic pitch (same logic as a trigger opener) |
| Negotiated, or handled pushback without caving | Agree, reframe, find a yes that works for both sides |
| Hit targets, quotas or volume goals | Comfort with activity metrics and pressure |
| Fundraising, recruiting, retail, hospitality, door-to-door, call centre, club sponsorships | Direct experience with rejection and short conversations with strangers |

Answer structure (about 45 seconds): honest gap, then one transferable story with a number, then how they are closing the gap (practicing with this method; the metrics they'll track: dials, connects, conversations, meetings), then enthusiasm for the role. Example close: "I haven't done outbound dials as my job yet, but I've spent years getting busy people to say yes to the next conversation, and I'm already practicing the phone part every day."

## Mode C. Role-play rules

Claude plays the prospect. Before starting, state in one line: persona, company, mood, difficulty (1 easy, 2 realistic, 3 hard), language. Default: difficulty 2.

While in character:
- Answer like a real busy person: short, sometimes distracted, sometimes interrupting. No helpful hints.
- Throw the reflex objection first at difficulty 2 and 3. At difficulty 3, add a second real objection and a gatekeeper at the start.
- Reward good moves realistically: a sharp problem statement gets a "...actually, yeah, that's a pain"; a generic pitch gets "not interested".
- Book the meeting only if they earned it. Hang up if they monologue past ~30 seconds without asking anything (difficulty 3).
- If they write "pause" or "time out", step out of character to help, then resume.
- In voice mode, keep prospect lines to one or two sentences.

After the call, step out of character and give a **scorecard**:

| Dimension | What good looks like | Score 1-5 |
|---|---|---|
| Opener | Name, company, honest reason within ~10s; permission or real-customer hook | |
| Relevance | Specific persona problem or trigger, plain language | |
| Brevity | Pitch under ~30s, then a question | |
| Listening | Paused after questions, used their words back | |
| Objections | Agreed, asked, bridged; no arguing, no "but" | |
| Ask | Specific meeting, purpose, length, two time options | |
| Lock-in | Confirmed email/time/attendees, or a clean secondary ask | |
| Delivery | Calm pace, "we/our customers", natural, right register | |

Then: the **one** thing to fix first, the exact line to try instead, and an offer to rerun at the same or higher difficulty. Track scores across rounds in the conversation so the user can see progress.

## Output: the call card

Keep it to one screen. This is what the caller has in front of them during the call.

```
CALL CARD: [Prospect name], [Title] @ [Company]   Lang: [EN/ES] [(tú/usted)]
Goal: book [20-min meeting with AE/demo] | Secondary: [right person / dated follow-up]

Trigger: [one line, source]          Proof: [real customer + result, source]
Opener: [chosen opener, 1-2 lines]
Problem prop: "[problem 1] or [problem 2]... sound familiar, or not really?"
Qualify (max 2): [question] / [question]
Likely objections, short answers:
  1. [objection] > [answer]
  2. [objection] > [answer]
  3. [objection] > [answer]
Ask: "[specific ask with two time slots]"
Voicemail (15s): "[...]"
Follow-up email subject: "[2-4 words]"
(unverified items marked)
```

For mode B with several accounts, produce one card per account and a short priority order (strongest trigger first).

## Reference data (benchmarks vary a lot by list quality and market)

- Gong Labs, 300M+ cold calls (2024): "heard the name tossed around" ~11.2% success and permission-based opener ~11.2%, versus much lower generic openers. Stating a reason for the call ~2.1x success; "Did I catch you at a bad time?" 0.9% vs 1.5% baseline (earlier 90k-call study).
- Gong: the top 5 objections are ~74% of all objections; dismissive ~49.5%, situational ~42.6%, existing solution ~7.9%.
- Gong: voicemails rarely get callbacks but roughly double email reply rate (about 2.7% to 5.9%); leave 1 to 2, never ask for a callback.
- Successful cold calls: rep talks ~55% of the time; longer (not shorter) monologues on successful calls (~53s vs ~25s), because a good pitch gets to finish. Successful calls use more "we".
- Connect rates range from ~5% on poor data to 13%+ for top reps or verified numbers; dial-to-meeting rates around 2% to 3% on average. Use these to set realistic expectations (dials, connects, conversations, meetings, meetings held).
- 30 Minutes to President's Club (Farrokh & Cegelski, *Cold Calling Sucks (And That's Why It Works)*): problem proposition over value proposition; agree-first objection handling; triple touch; 30 seconds to disqualify, 30 seconds to research.
- Spain: Ley 11/2022 art. 66.1.b; AEPD Circular 1/2023; Orden TDF/149/2025 (from 7 June 2025, no unsolicited commercial calls from mobile numbers). Not legal advice; defer to employer compliance.

If the user asks for current numbers, re-check with a web search, since benchmarks shift every year.

## Never

- Invent customers, stats, triggers or experience (the user's or the company's).
- Write a robotic word-for-word script without beats. Give beats plus example lines.
- Use deceptive openers (fake familiarity, fake referrals, "returning your call").
- Keep calling someone who said not to.
